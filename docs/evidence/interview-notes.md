# Raw Interview Notes

**Project:** VeriTalk
**Methodology:** Semi-structured interviews conducted between Oct 18 and Oct 21, 2026.
**Objective:** Understand current fraud defense mechanisms, pain points, and user expectations for an AI-based risk assessment tool.
**Questions:** see [00-interview-guide.md](00-interview-guide.md).

---

## Session 1
* **Date:** 2026-10-18
* **Participant:** Tassimov Arsen
* **Role:** University Student
* **Key Points:**
  * Gets scam calls 1-2 times a month, but his parents get them weekly.
  * Uses Truecaller, but complains that scammers now use local virtual numbers (spoofing) that bypass the spam filter.
  * *Pain point:* Truecaller doesn't explain *why* it's spam, and often misses new numbers. 
  * Willing to use speakerphone mode if he suspects a scam, especially when helping his parents navigate a call.
  * *Privacy:* Demands strict local processing or immediate deletion of audio.

## Session 2
* **Date:** 2026-10-19
* **Participant:** Erkebulan Zhan
* **Role:** University Student
* **Key Points:**
  * Frequently receives calls from "bank security" speaking Russian and sometimes switching to Kazakh.
  * Relies purely on common sense (e.g., when they ask for SMS codes).
  * Uninstalled caller ID apps because of false positives (blocked legitimate food delivery drivers).
  * Loves the idea of an app that analyzes what is *said*. 
  * *Speakerphone:* "Usually, if I think it's a scam, I already put it on speaker to mock them, so using an app on speakerphone is fine."

## Session 3
* **Date:** 2026-10-19
* **Participant:** Zalel Mansur
* **Role:** University Student
* **Key Points:**
  * Almost fell victim to a scam during exam week because he was stressed and the caller knew his full name.
  * Does not use anti-spam apps because they drain battery and require too many permissions.
  * *Pain point:* Cannot quickly figure out if the person on the line is a real official due to psychological pressure.
  * Needs the solution to be extremely simple—a massive red warning screen.
  * *Privacy:* Cares less about privacy, cares more about having a reliable warning system.

## Session 4
* **Date:** 2026-10-20
* **Participant:** Kenzhegaliev Ali
* **Role:** IT Operator
* **Key Points:**
  * Refuses to use Truecaller or GetContact due to contact-book harvesting. 
  * Currently uses the built-in iOS/Android "Silence Unknown Callers," which makes him miss important calls from recruiters or couriers.
  * Would prefer a feature to *upload* a recorded audio file for analysis after the call, rather than real-time streaming, to ensure mic privacy.
  * App must show *exactly* which words triggered the scam warning (explainability).

## Session 5
* **Date:** 2026-10-20
* **Participant:** Rauan Ali
* **Role:** University Student
* **Key Points:**
  * Gets spam calls often and usually ignores unknown numbers.
  * Realized that blocking all unknown numbers is not sustainable as graduation approaches (expecting job interviews).
  * Highly interested in real-time analysis but wants it to be fast. If the app takes 30 seconds to analyze, the scammer might already have convinced the victim.
  * *Privacy:* Audio must not be linked to his phone number in any database.

## Session 6
* **Date:** 2026-10-21
* **Participant:** Ermek Tairhan
* **Role:** University Student
* **Key Points:**
  * Mentioned a specific case where a scammer started in Kazakh to build trust, then switched to Russian for "technical banking terms".
  * Feels that an AI that provides a text transcript of the suspicious phrases would be the most convincing way to prove it's a scam.
  * Finds speakerphone mode acceptable when at home, but awkward in public spaces.
  * *Privacy:* Wants a clear "Audio Deleted" confirmation after every session.
